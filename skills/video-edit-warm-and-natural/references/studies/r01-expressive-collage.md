## Composition, cut, and grade

The presenter remains the main anchor. Most objects use the space above or beside the head, while captions are lower. The green inserts offer space that the room footage cannot. Slight card rotations and overlap suggest an assembled scrapbook rather than aligned software windows. Apparent crop changes occur across edits; the broadly stable room geometry suggests a static base shot with reframing, but the original camera setup is unknown.

Warm footage and cooler green inserts create separation. Cream objects remain high-contrast against both. Red is used for editorial intervention; brighter icon colours carry creative possibility. Neither a precise palette nor a LUT should be copied from a compressed sample.

No convincing subject-occlusion requirement appears in the inspected sequence. Most graphics can remain screen-space layers. Do not add rotoscoping or tracking simply to recreate complexity.

## Recreate the principle, adapt the direction

Use **containment → interruption → expansion** when the story involves an unrealised idea becoming possible. In another project, a constrained light pattern could open into a wider image, a sketch could become a functional prototype, or an isolated rhythm could gain complementary layers. The principle survives without a thought bubble or paper.

Use a **source-to-object handoff** when the film must explain its own process. A music documentary could reduce an archival clip into a timeline; an instrument demo could isolate a recorded waveform. Keep temporal continuity where it matters.

Use a **developing record** only when the values or states have meaning. Invented receipt counts are not evidence. Avoid the original's most crowded moments when the brief needs restraint. Check that secondary cards, handwriting, and caption emphasis never compete with the main thought.
