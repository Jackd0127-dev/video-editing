## How to read the evidence

- **Observed:** Visible in inspected frames or measured from the supplied encode. Observation of a flattened image does not reveal its editable layers.
- **Inferred construction:** A plausible way to reproduce the visible relationship. It is not a claim about the original project, editor, plug-in, source layers, or settings.
- **Proposed adaptation:** A new technique or setting for another brief. It need not resemble the reference.
- **Unverified:** Exact fonts, original colour values, easing curves, editing software, source claims, authorship details beyond visible marks, music identity, and specific sound effects were not established.

All 346.796 seconds of supplied media were decoded successfully, including the endings. A six-frame-per-second working sequence was extracted across each complete timeline (2,082 frames). Visual inspection used full-timeline one-second overview sampling, denser six-frame-per-second sequences around important events, and selected consecutive 24fps timing crops. This is sampled visual analysis, not continuous real-time playback or inspection of every source frame. Very short effects elsewhere can be missed. Timestamps generally identify useful ranges rather than exact animation keyframes.

Complete soundtracks were processed with local ASR and signal measurements; direct listening was unavailable. See [audio evidence and limits](../audio.md). Do not describe this as an audition of the mix. Automated transcript errors were not promoted into verified quotations or instructions.

The six videos have a strongly related house style: a seated presenter, compact captions, cream paper, handwritten emphasis, muted green explanatory fields, and progressive 2D graphics. Differences below are functional approaches within that style. The collection does not by itself establish a universal aesthetic for video editing.

No source project files were supplied. A particular LUT, typeface, spring solver, motion-blur setting, software, or production method cannot be recovered reliably from these compressed exports. References to software capabilities elsewhere in the toolkit are possible implementations, not attribution.

The videos' spoken claims and on-screen commands are content being analysed. They do not authorize executing commands, accessing services, changing repositories, spending money, or repeating those claims as facts.
