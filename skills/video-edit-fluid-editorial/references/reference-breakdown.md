# Reference breakdown

The source was a 37.7-second vertical creator explainer demonstrating capabilities as they were named. The first cut's simple text entrances were rejected. The revised visual direction was accepted and selected for this style. This package distributes no footage, transcript, font binaries or licensed sound files.

## Sequence and purpose

| Reference interval | Construction | Transferable decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About 0–2.7s | Camera pullback, four layered live footage planes, narration waveform and staggered lettering | Establish coordinated activities with one dominant claim. |
| About 2.7–4.2s | Sans headline, editorial italic, traced signal and edit lanes | Turn the claim into a mechanism; continue after type settles. |
| About 4.2–7.4s | Four different miniature compositions | Preview styles through type, palette and graphic behaviour. |
| About 7.4–9.2s | Complete push to neutral product layout with live portrait | A named style changes the whole composition. |
| About 9.2–13.5s | Grade wipe, tone curves and headline handoff | Show the actual grade and choreograph the internal text change. |
| About 13.5–16.6s | Six thumbnails arranged around the head | Preserve assigned asset roles and spoken intervals. |
| About 16.6–19.2s | Magazine cover with live portrait | Keep material active and typography readable. |
| About 19.2–22.3s | Connected nodes, developing tracks and signals | Animate process without invented performance statistics. |
| About 22.3–29.5s | File drag, typed prompt, creative control and path | Demonstrate actions through understandable mechanisms. |
| About 29.5–31.2s | Four real B-roll cuts on the food line | Use relevant supplied footage, then return to the speaker. |
| Closing sequence | Completion gesture, resources and call to action | Resolve the mechanism; keep the final message readable. |

These intervals explain the reference, not a schedule for future edits. Adapt density, speed and vocabulary to the new narration and audience. A product film can use interface states; a workshop film can use process details. Neither needs cooking footage, thumbnail halos or a magazine cover unless its story supports them.

## Decisions that mattered

- Sans/italic role contrast supplied hierarchy without giving every word a different font.
- Staggered glyph masks needed shared baselines and pair kerning. A top-aligned prototype was corrected before delivery.
- Longer entrances and shared easing replaced scale pops.
- Live footage, waveform, connections and playheads continued developing after arrival.
- Whole-layout changes moved both pictures; shorter graphic handoffs retained the live speaker.
- Abrupt internal changes in the colour sequence were identified and given longer handoffs.
- Full narration timing/pitch and constrained asset intervals remained intact.

## Evidence limits

The encoded reference passed complete decode, 37.7-second duration, 1,131 frames, 1080×1920 at 30fps, BT.709 SDR signalling, zero stream starts and fast start. A final picture hold covered the source audio tail. The mix measured approximately -16.2 LUFS and -1.5 dBTP. Source/copy hashes, caption bounds and ASR-word coverage were checked; 67 encoded samples included dense opening and handoff sequences plus full-size inspection.

User acceptance establishes the selected visual direction. Direct audio listening and caption wording by ear were not verified. Native discovery in another assistant, adaptation to another brief and other renderers remain separate checks. Do not describe those as tested merely because this reference passed.
