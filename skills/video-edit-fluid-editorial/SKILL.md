# Layered Motion Video Editing

Make the opening feel designed from the first frame. Combine the speaker, relevant footage, expressive type and a developing graphic mechanism; let the motion carry the explanation forward. This is one selected style, not the default for every video.

Preserve the brief's message and asset boundaries. The visual language is reusable; the reference's words, exact layouts, cooking sequence and timing are not a template for other stories.

## Visual grammar

- **Type:** Pair a strong geometric sans with a high-contrast editorial italic. Use the italic for selected important words, the sans for clarity and a magazine serif when a cover is appropriate. The reference used actual Avenir Black/Heavy/Roman, Bauer Bodoni Italic and Didot faces. Use available fonts with appropriate rights; do not bundle those binaries or synthesize missing weights.
- **Palette:** Cream, warm gold and grounded dark ink suit warm footage. A neutral field with cobalt accents can mark a clean product demonstration. Adapt these roles to the footage and brand; a dark background is not a requirement.
- **Layers:** A live speaker anchors the story. Relevant footage fragments, headlines, connections and timelines occupy separate visual planes. Give each layer a job and preserve faces, gestures and caption space.
- **Motion:** Longer masked slides, staggered lettering, continuous camera travel and linked handoffs replace repeated short scale pops. Give important graphics arrival, development, readable hold and departure phases.

The optional [style properties](references/style-properties.json) record roles and starting ranges. Read [motion construction](references/motion-construction.md) when implementing masks, glyph layout, transitions or signals. The [reference breakdown](references/reference-breakdown.md) explains the original sequence and evidence limits.

## Build the hook as a sequence

Start with visible content; avoid spending the first spoken word on an empty entrance. Choose complementary activities: a bounded camera pullback, moving footage planes, staggered type, an actual narration waveform, a traced signal or developing edit tracks.

Land those activities on the narration. Keep one dominant idea readable while secondary layers develop. Introduce the main statement, its qualifying words, then the mechanism or result. Density should support the claim, not hide the speaker or obscure the sentence.

Footage planes may use the current recording to demonstrate raw versus edited material. Do not repurpose thumbnails, B-roll or reference clips outside the role the user assigned. Style-reference videos are craft evidence, not automatic permission to insert their footage.

## Keep the picture alive after entrance

Develop information: trace a connection, move a playhead, type the next line, advance a waveform or keep a magazine portrait live. Slight drift can supply depth after settling; continuous motion does not mean wiggling every word or preventing a readable hold.

Share timing across cooperating layers. A headline and diagram should feel part of one movement. Use different depths or arrival times where hierarchy needs them; avoid unrelated easing on every object.

When the speaker names a style or capability, demonstrate it at that moment. A clean layout can replace the whole composition; a grade can wipe across the actual footage; a thumbnail halo can surround the head; an infographic can develop connections. A label alone is not the demonstration.

## Choreograph handoffs

Plan outgoing and incoming states together. Use a complete picture push for a whole-layout change; use a shorter graphic handoff over a continuing speaker. Maintain live footage through the handoff where possible.

Inspect internal changes too: changing headlines within one section can create a hard cut even when section transitions are smooth. Avoid unintended blank intervals, unreadable overlaps and premature departures. Asset-specific timing restrictions take precedence over extending a transition.

## Captions and sound

Group captions by meaning, reserve final phrase positions, then reveal words into a shared baseline. Use a readable medium/heavy face, soft shadow and restrained active-word emphasis. Check important text at phone size. Large motion type can carry selected phrases; provide complete sidecar captions when the picture omits those words.

Keep narration timing and pitch unless changes are authorized. Use recorded cues for visible arrivals, travel, typing, drawn marks or confirmations. Align audible activity with the gesture; file start is not necessarily audible onset. A busy opening does not require a cue for every glyph. Music is optional when appropriate to the brief and available rights.

## Render and verify

Use an available editing or compositing system. These instructions require no paid provider, native template host or particular renderer. The reference used local Pillow/FFmpeg. Respect the user's generation preferences and release authority.

Check the encoded video, not only layout stills. Inspect dense opening and handoff sequences, readable holds, common baselines, masks, face clearance and phone-scale captions. Check that continuing motion serves a purpose. Validate saved-file duration, frame count, colour, stream timing and complete decode; measure encoded loudness/true peak and audition when possible.

For mixed phone HDR and SDR footage, honour rotation and tone-map before compositing. Set output colour conversion and signalling explicitly. Preserve originals and keep editable timing, source pointers and selected-asset rights with the private project. Distribute only original guidance and assets permitted for redistribution.

Report rendered, technically verified, visually inspected and auditioned states separately. Acceptance of the visual style does not prove an unauditioned mix or authorize publishing private media.
