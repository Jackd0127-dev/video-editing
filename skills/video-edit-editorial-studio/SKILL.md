# Video Edit Editorial Studio

Combine expressive editorial typography with a clean demonstration stage. The speaker carries the story; words land on speech, the camera makes room for the explanation, and graphic objects develop into the next idea. This is an adaptable style, not a compulsory sequence or a replacement for the user's brief.

This style was distilled from an approved vertical creator edit. Its defining combination is strong Avenir-style statements, Bodoni-style italic emphasis, warm footage, white explanatory fields, cobalt functional accents and continuously developing graphics. It shares editorial roots with Fluid Editorial but gives clean stages and a movable presenter window a larger role.

## Choose and prepare

Use for creator explainers, capability demonstrations and compact teaching sequences. Keep a natural talking-head composition when the story does not benefit from a stage. Do not add a magazine, orbit, grade wipe or numbered process merely because the reference contains one.

Identify the main recording, message, allowed cuts, asset roles and delivery format. Preserve wording, narration order and source timing unless the user authorizes changes. Reference clips guide craft; thumbnails and B-roll appear only where their assigned subject belongs. Use licensed local assets and respect the user's generation preferences.

Read the [style properties](references/style-properties.json) for measured starting values. They describe the approved example and should scale with canvas, font metrics and reading time.

## Typography is the spine

Use an actual heavy geometric sans for statements, its readable heavy/roman companions for labels and captions, and a high-contrast italic for selected important words. The reference used Avenir LT Std Black/Heavy/Roman and Bauer Bodoni Std Italic. Didot LT Std Headline was reserved for a magazine masthead. These are verified reference faces, not bundled assets or mandatory purchases.

Load the real faces before capture. Preserve native kerning, natural word spaces and shared baselines. Keep tracking moderate; leave generous room for italic overhangs, descenders and soft shadows. Test licensed substitutes on representative phrases before committing to the whole edit.

Give primary words spoken-beat masked rises; give italic accents horizontal reveals; use a shared vertical slot for a meaningful phrase replacement. Supporting copy travels a small distance and settles. Avoid applying the same blur, scale pop or diagonal fade to every line. Hold completed phrases long enough to read. Read [typography and motion](references/typography-motion.md) when laying out or animating text.

## Build a visible, layered hook

Begin close on the speaker and pull back quickly, with the first dominant statement already visible. Keep eyes and mouth framed throughout the movement. Develop two or three complementary layers: qualifying words, relevant portrait planes, a file/result mechanism or a genuine narration waveform. Secondary activity supports one readable claim.

A restrained recorded riser can build beneath the hook and release at its rhetorical turn. Do not let the opening entrance consume the first spoken word or let every layer demand an equally loud sound.

## Make space for demonstrations

Move the continuing recording into a rounded corner window while a white sheet expands into the composition. Choose the corner from face, hand and text clearance; the reference used lower-left and lower-right positions, not a universal corner rule. Keep source playback synchronized during the move.

Use the resulting space to demonstrate the spoken idea: a short typographic statement, a grade on actual footage, an editorial cover, an orbit of relevant media, independent diagrams or process steps. Clean sections use open white space, dark ink, restrained cobalt and soft depth. A plain white interruption can emphasize a short phrase without keeping the presenter visible in every frame.

Plan outgoing and incoming layouts together. Reuse a sheet, camera rectangle, baseline, icon, palette or graphic position across the boundary. Shared geometry creates continuity; only call it a literal morph if the object actually persists. Restore the full speaker when the story returns to presence or B-roll. Read [scene construction](references/scene-construction.md) for these devices.

## Keep graphics developing

Give motion a visible job after arrival: trace a ring, vary illustrative bars, open a palette fan, type a prompt, extend a rule or emphasize the current step. Keep labels steady and readable. Multiple independent graphics can coexist without a large enclosing square or dashboard chrome.

When moving into a process, let existing diagrams compress toward the next section's markers while the new heading and steps arrive. Use a coherent shared clock with small offsets, not unrelated easing on each item. Avoid unsupported statistics; decorative motion bars illustrate motion unless they encode real data.

A grade demonstration follows measured gesture positions, visibly develops its strength and reverses to the original look when that demonstration ends. An orbit surrounds the head using a real curved path and depth ordering. A magazine uses meaningful hierarchy and a permitted portrait. Adapt or omit each mechanism to the new story.

## Captions, sound and finish

Group captions by meaning, reserve the completed phrase width and reveal words into a common baseline. Use heavy sans, a small vertical glide, and restrained gold over warm footage or cobalt on white for active words. Soft shadows supply separation over footage; avoid a jagged black stroke. Let large motion type carry selected lines without duplicating them above and below, while supplying complete sidecar captions.

Narration leads the mix. Use licensed recorded accents for useful arrivals, travel and confirmations. If sound is unchanged in a revision, preserve the complete original audio payload. Do not substitute a generic cue list for alignment to the final visuals. Read [sound and finishing](references/sound-finishing.md) for timing and export checks.

Remotion is a good fit when available: frame-based React type, SVG, continuous video and explicit font loading. The approved picture was rendered in Remotion; an earlier Hyperframes study was separate. Another capable compositor is acceptable. No paid provider, generated media or particular installation is required by this skill. Optional dependency-free [motion helpers](assets/motion.mjs) expose the core easing and poses for adaptation.

Inspect the encoded video at phone size and native resolution, including arrivals, completed holds and adjacent handoff frames. Check letter edges, italic clipping, caption space, face/hand clearance, colour restoration and unintended blank flashes. Validate timing, colour signalling, frame count, complete decode and audio; distinguish sampled inspection and measurements from real-time playback or direct listening. Keep originals and editable source pointers private. Creative approval never implies permission to publish media or redistribute fonts.

The [approved reference breakdown](references/approved-reference.md) records what was observed, measured and accepted, without supplying the private media or fixing future videos to its chapter order.
